Design a News Feed Like Twitter

The central question is when to do the work: when a post is written, or when a feed is read. The answer changes with follower count, which is why every real feed is a hybrid.

By Arman Ajabkhani, Senior Backend Engineer · Updated 31 Oct 2026

The short answer

Requirements. Publish posts with text and media, follow and unfollow, and read a paginated feed of followed accounts, newest first; the feed loads in under 200 ms, a new post may take a few seconds to show up, and reads far outnumber writes.

The design. Publishing stores the post once, then fan-out workers push its ID into the feed cache of every follower, a Redis list capped at about 500 IDs. Accounts above about 100K followers skip the fan-out, and the feed service merges their recent posts in at read time: the hybrid of push and pull that large feeds use. A read takes the IDs from the cache, fills in the posts from the post and user caches, and pages with a cursor, never an offset.

Interview prompt

Design the home timeline for a social network: users follow others and see their posts, newest first.

Requirements and estimates

Functional requirements

  • Publish a post with text and media.
  • Follow and unfollow users.
  • View a paginated feed of posts from followed users.

Non-functional requirements

  • Feed loads in under 200 ms.
  • A new post may take a few seconds to appear (eventual consistency).
  • Read heavy and highly available.

Estimates

Feed reads  300M DAU × 10 opens ÷ 86,400 s     ≈ 35,000 reads/s
New posts   300M DAU × 2 posts ÷ 86,400 s      ≈ 7,000 posts/s
Fan-out     7,000 posts/s × 200 followers      ≈ 1.4M feed inserts/s if we push
Feed cache  300M users × 500 post IDs × 8 B    ≈ 1.2 TB of IDs in memory

Hybrid is the standard answer: push for ordinary accounts, and for accounts above a threshold (say 100K followers) skip fan-out. At read time the feed service merges the precomputed feed with fresh posts from the few celebrities the reader follows. Skip fan-out to users inactive for 30 days too, and build their feed by pull when they come back.

Feed pagination with a cursor, and ranking

Paginate with a cursor (the last post ID seen), never with OFFSET. New posts arriving at the top shift every offset, so page two by offset repeats items; a cursor stays stable. Ranked feeds fetch a few hundred candidates, score them by affinity, recency and engagement, and cache the ranked page for a few minutes.

What the interviewer probes next

A user follows someone new. When do old posts appear?

Backfill asynchronously: fetch the new followee’s recent posts and merge them into the follower’s cached feed. Unfollow is handled by filtering at read time and cleaning the cache lazily.

The feed cache node dies. Now what?

The feed cache is derived data. Rebuild a user’s feed on demand by pulling recent posts from their followees, then resume pushing. Replicate the cache so a single node loss rarely forces this.

How are deleted or edited posts handled?

Feeds store IDs only. Hydration reads the current post, and deleted posts are filtered out at read time, so nothing needs to be rewritten across millions of feeds.

How do media uploads fit in?

Clients upload directly to object storage with a pre-signed URL, a worker creates thumbnails, and the CDN serves them. Posts keep only media IDs.
